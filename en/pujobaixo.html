From a WhatsApp experiment to a scalable local network.

  • ProjectOwn product, evolving
  • RoleUX Researcher · Product Owner
  • SectorMobility · Community · Marketplace

Pujobaixo began as a field experiment: a WhatsApp group of people who regularly made the trip between Berguedà and Barcelona, to observe how supply, demand and matches emerged in a real context.

The challenge was to turn those learnings into a network able to handle more trips without losing the simplicity and flexibility of the original coordination.

The goal wasn’t to introduce a new behavior, but to understand a practice that already existed and study how to turn it into a more useful, scalable local network.

The challenge

The question was whether informal coordination on WhatsApp could evolve into a digital product able to serve a wider community. Before designing an interface, we had to understand what system was already emerging within the community.

How do you turn informal coordination of shared trips into a scalable network, keeping it simple and reducing the effort of finding a match?

The experiment

The WhatsApp group worked as a field experiment and a low-fidelity service prototype. Instead of asking whether people would use a shared-mobility platform, we created a space where coordination could happen and observed how its members behaved.

It confirmed there were people willing to share a ride and split costs when they found a suitable match. And it revealed the limits of using a chronological conversation as a system:

  • It was hard to know which trips were still available.
  • Some posts didn’t include all the necessary details.
  • Earlier trips were buried under new messages.
  • There was no combined view of supply and demand.

Patterns and insights

The habit existed, but the network didn’t yet

The opportunity wasn’t to create the behavior, but to connect it in a more visible, recurring and scalable system.

The main friction was finding the match

The difficulty lay in locating a compatible trip, checking whether it was still active and filling in the details to coordinate it.

Scaling required structure, not complexity

The product had to organize the information without turning a flexible practice into a rigid process.

Evidence, insight and decision

EvidenceThere were recurring trips and a willingness to share them, but finding trips in a conversation took too much effort.
InsightThe habit already existed. What was missing was a structure to connect more supply and demand and make it visible.
DecisionA digital network that centralizes active trips, makes matching easier and keeps WhatsApp for the final coordination.

Key decisions

Pujobaixo doesn’t replace the dynamic that already worked: it only structures the parts that took the most effort.

Separating supply and demand

«Ofereixo» (“I’m offering”) for drivers with seats and «Necessito» (“I need”) for people looking for a ride. You can tell at a glance what kind of post you’re looking at.

Structuring the trip information

Each post groups everything needed to assess a match: origin, destination, date, time, seats and extra details. Fewer conversations to fill in the gaps.

Centralizing active trips

Trips no longer depend on a group’s chronological order and become searchable posts, with a clear view of what’s available at any moment.

Keeping WhatsApp for the final coordination

Once there’s a match, people contact each other directly on WhatsApp. It narrows the product’s initial scope and keeps a channel the community already knows.

Giving control over availability

Each person manages and cancels their own trips, which keeps the information up to date and reduces uncertainty about what’s still active.

Repeating without starting from scratch

The latest features reduce the effort for people who make the trip often: post the return trip in the same form, mark a trip as recurring so it’s posted only once, and get an alert if a compatible trip appears (within ±2 h, same day, adjustable).

The alert arrives by email, personalized: who posted, the route, their time versus the one you asked for, and a button to get in touch.

Outcome

Pujobaixo turned a WhatsApp coordination experiment into a working product that centralizes the supply and demand of trips between Berguedà and Barcelona. The value wasn’t in inventing a new behavior, but in reducing the cost of coordinating one that already existed.

Next phase: measuring how the network really behaves, with trips offered and requested, matches by direction and time slot, time to first contact and trips that actually happen.

Scaling a community practice doesn’t always mean replacing it: it means observing what already works and structuring only the friction.
