K Rooms as the single source of information and management.

  • ProjectCustom commission for Konvent (Cal Rosal), 2026
  • RoleProduct designer and product builder
  • PlatformResponsive web app (mobile and desktop)
  • MVP development15 days

Konvent is a cultural center in Cal Rosal, run on a volunteer basis by the people who live there. A very wide range of people pass through the house: permanent residents, temporary residents, event participants, visiting artists, and family and friends.

Someone already took care of deciding who slept where. What was missing was a single place to manage all that information, with quick lookups and less room for error.

40rooms
2,000residents a year
90events a year

The starting decision

Abandon the fragmented model (Excel, paper and everyone’s own codes) and build its opposite: a single place where the information lives, updated instantly.

That decision forced four questions:

  • Who is going to use this? Not a dedicated team, but volunteers in their free time. The tool has to remove work and learning, not add them.
  • Are all stays the same? No. There are three types of stay, sharing the same root but with different needs.
  • What happens when volume grows? A form that works for one person is a burden for thirty. A second path is needed.
  • Is a room a fixed piece of data? No. Its status changes from day to day, so managing it is an operational task, not an administrative one.

What came before

Stays were managed with a spreadsheet, its printed plan and hand-written labels on every door. Each tool held part of the information, and what was noted in one did not appear in the others.

  • Several sources instead of one. Every change meant updating several media by hand.
  • No source was reliable. The Excel fell out of date and the paper did not capture the changes.
  • Everyone used their own codes. Understanding a note depended on who had written it.
  • Keeping it up to date took time from the volunteers.

One room, two people

Two people were organizing two different events. One kept their part on paper; the other, in the Excel. The same room ended up assigned to two guests at once, and the error was discovered with both of them at the door, suitcase in hand.

It was not an anecdote: it was the most visible symptom of fragmentation. For that reason, K Rooms proposes a single circuit: whoever manages enters or edits the information just once, and the data and the rooms update instantly.

From a generic product to a tailored one

The first ideas followed generic patterns: as much data as possible was included so that usability tests could validate which of it was actually used. Three redesigns came out of that.

1 · Only the data people actually use

A card with horizontal scroll showed daily and weekly occupancy: it duplicated the information without adding anything new. Now there is a single level: three indicators for the day and a visual date selector.

2 · A map, the way people already solved it

Assigning with dropdowns by floor was abstract and slow: every assignment was a blind click. An isometric view was tried, and it added nothing. The top-down view is understood in the first second because it is the one they already used on paper.

3 · A backoffice far from the urgency of day-to-day work

Opening or closing a room or adding a mattress happens throughout the day, and going through a backoffice was friction. Capacity is now edited from the room card; the backoffice stays for structural tasks.

The simplicity of a complex flow

Registering a stay starts with an innocent question, “Who is coming?”, and the tree branches out: adults, children and babies, with or without a bed; continuous or non-continuous dates; free and taken rooms. Any combination fits in the same flow, and so do changes: someone arrives later, leaves earlier or did not need a bed.

Complexity is not removed: it is made quick to use.

When the flow does not scale

The form works for one family. For a residency of 20 or 30 people, the list already exists in an email, an Excel or a message: you paste it, and each line becomes a record with its name and dates.

A product with no learning curve

Not everyone knows how to use an Excel, but almost everyone recognizes the vocabulary of any app: lists, buttons, selectors, icons. K Rooms is built with that vocabulary, and it works the same on mobile as on desktop.

What was not built

What was decided not to do explains the product as much as what was done.

Ruled outWhyWhat was done instead
Native iOS/Android appFor 3 or 4 people, maintaining two apps is not worth it.Responsive web app.
Own backend on the PiSeveral people edit at once and need to see changes in real time.Firebase as the data layer.
Email and password sign-upThe people who manage already know each other.Invite-only access.
Push notificationsThe problem was fragmented information, not a lack of alerts.The single source itself.
Google Calendar integrationCopying information to another place brings back the original problem.The map as the only operational view.
Analytics dashboardThere is not yet enough usage volume to justify it.Nothing. Phase 2, if needed.

It is not a SaaS. It is not a mobile app. It is not a commercial booking system. It is not an analytics dashboard.

Outcome

1single source of truth, shared and always up to date
15days of MVP development
0licenses and subscriptions

Before, the information lived scattered across a spreadsheet, a printed plan and door labels. Today it lives in one single place: whoever manages enters or edits once, and the data and the rooms update instantly for the 3 or 4 people who coordinate the work.

What was learned

  • The single source, first. Before adding features, close off the possibility of two versions of the same information.
  • Open another path when a flow does not scale, instead of polishing it.
  • Look at how users solve the problem themselves: the top-down map picks up the plan that already existed in the Excel.
  • Data that helps decide: what is needed today, not everything that can be measured.
